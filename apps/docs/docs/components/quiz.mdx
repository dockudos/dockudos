Use `Quiz` to let readers test themselves at the end of a page or tutorial. Mark the right answers with `correct`. A
question with one right answer is a single choice; with several, readers select all that apply.

<Quiz id="quiz-demo" title="Check your understanding">
  <Question
    text="Where does the URL of a page come from?"
    explanation="The URL is the file path without the extension, so docs/guides/seo.mdx is served at /guides/seo."
  >
    <Answer>The title in the frontmatter</Answer>
    <Answer correct>The path of the file</Answer>
    <Answer>The order in docs.json</Answer>
  </Question>
  <Question text="Which of these are components?">
    <Answer correct>Callout</Answer>
    <Answer>docs.json</Answer>
    <Answer correct>Tabs</Answer>
  </Question>
</Quiz>

```mdx
<Quiz id="urls" title="Check your understanding">
  <Question
    text="Where does the URL of a page come from?"
    explanation="The URL is the file path without the extension."
  >
    <Answer>The title in the frontmatter</Answer>
    <Answer correct>The path of the file</Answer>
  </Question>
</Quiz>
```

Readers choose, press **Check answer**, and see whether they were right, the correct answers and your explanation. **Try
again** redoes one question; **Start again** clears the whole quiz. When every question is checked, a score appears.

## Saving answers

With an `id`, checked answers are saved in the reader's browser (`localStorage`), so the score is there when they come
back. Without one, nothing is saved and a reload starts again. The `id` must be unique on the page; changing it starts a
fresh quiz. Answers are kept per question text; give a `Question` an `id` if you expect to reword it.

A `Question` can also be used on its own, outside a `Quiz`. It gives feedback but has no score and saves nothing.

<Callout type="note">
  A quiz runs in the reader's browser, so the right answers are in the page. It
  is for learning, not for grading.
</Callout>

A `Question` must be a direct child of the `Quiz` to count towards the score, and its answers direct children of the
`Question`. Text between questions is shown as it is.

## Accessibility and printing

Each question is a `fieldset` with its text as the legend, and the choices are native radio buttons or checkboxes, so the
keyboard works as in any form. The result is never color alone: right and wrong answers get a check or cross and are
named for screen readers, and "Correct." or "Not quite." is announced after checking.

On paper there is nothing to press, so every right answer is marked "(correct)" and every explanation is shown. Buttons and
the score are left out.
